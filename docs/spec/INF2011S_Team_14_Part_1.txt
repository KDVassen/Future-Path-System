1.       INTRODUCTION

     OVERVIEW OF SPECIFICATION
     Every year, large numbers of Grade 11 and Grade 12 learners in South Africa must choose a
     tertiary study path with only fragmented, inconsistent information to guide them, a university
     website here, a teacher's informal opinion there, outdated pamphlets, or second-hand advice
     from parents and peers. Because schools often have only one career advisor responsible for
     large numbers of learners, most learners never get a structured, individualised check of whether
     their current subjects and marks support the qualifications they are interested in. As
     documented in the project case study, the result is late discovery of admission mismatches,
     missed funding opportunities, and study choices that are not well aligned with a learner's actual
     academic profile or interests.

     Future Path addresses this gap directly. It is a standalone, Windows Forms-based advising
     application that lets a learner capture their grade, subjects, marks and interests, and then uses
     locally stored eligibility rules and a weighted scoring model to indicate whether they are a Strong
     Match, Possible Match, Not Currently Eligible, or require More Information for a selected
     qualification ,along with a plain-language explanation of the outcome, alternative programme
     suggestions, relevant funding information, and an exportable advisory summary the learner can
     share with a parent, teacher or career advisor.


     CONTEXT & SCOPE OF SYSTEM SPECIFICATION
     This design specification builds directly on the objectives and requirements established during
     the analysis phase: the system must operate entirely offline, using only local business rules and
     locally stored data, with no external AI services, APIs or cloud platforms involved at any point; it
     will be built on a three-tier architecture; and its advising outcomes will always be explainable,
     since Future Path is intended to provide preliminary guidance rather than official admission
     decisions. These requirements are treated as non-negotiable inputs to every design decision that
     follows in this document.

    Narrative:

    The diagram groups Future Path into its three mandated architectural layers: Presentation,
    Business Logic and Data with dependencies flowing from the Presentation Layer down through
    the Business Logic Layer to the Data Layer. This specification covers the full vertical slice of the
    system end-to-end, because the case study requires every learner-facing feature (profile
    capture, interest questionnaire, eligibility checking, funding lookup, report export) to be
    underpinned by rule-based logic and locally stored data at every step, a partial-layer design
    would not satisfy that requirement. Within the Business Logic Layer, the eligibility and scoring
    engine is treated as the system's critical-path component: it is the dependency that every other
    business service and presentation form ultimately relies on to produce a usable outcome, and it
    is the component most directly tied to the project's core value proposition of explainable, rule-
    based advising. Design and implementation effort is therefore prioritised on this engine and its
    supporting data structures (subjects, programme requirements, interest categories) before the
    surrounding presentation forms and secondary services (funding lookup, report export) are
    finalised.

    OVERVIEW OF SPECIFICATION
    In-Scope:

        ● A standalone Windows Forms desktop application using a local relational database, built
        on a three-tier architecture.

        ● Learner profile creation, subject and mark capture, and a short interest questionnaire.

        ● Rule-based eligibility checking and a weighted recommendation engine producing a Strong
        Match, Possible Match, Not Currently Eligible Match, More Information Needed outcome,
        with an explanation.

        ● Suggestion of alternative degree programmes when a learner does not meet the
        requirements for their first-choice qualification.

        ● Display of locally stored funding information linked to a selected faculty or degree.

        ● Export of a learner advisory summary report, plus two further administrative/advisory
        reports (an aggregate eligibility/interest report and a not-currently-eligible exception
        report).

        ● Authentication and role-based access control distinguishing Learner, Career Advisor and
        System Administrator functionality.



    Out of scope for this project:

        ● Any connection to ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, other online AI services, external APIs, cloud
        platforms or a web server, all advising logic must run locally.

        ● Making or replacing official university admission decisions; Future Path provides
        preliminary guidance only.

        ● Live or real-time funding and admissions data, funding and requirement information is
        locally stored sample data, not pulled from the internet.

        ● Submission of funding or university applications on the learner's behalf.

         ● Support for multiple universities, detailed career pathway mapping beyond the interest
        questionnaire, parent/teacher login, automated application checklists, closing-date
        reminders, multilingual support, or natural-language interaction, these are noted as possible
        future extensions but are not part of the current build.

2.      USER INTERFACE & DIALOGUE DESIGN
The user interface for the Future Path system must be designed to ensure that Learners, Career
Advisors, and Administrators can navigate the system logically and interact with its major functions.
The interface design focuses on form-to-form navigation, specific screens required for profiling,
subject and mark capturing, interest questionnaire, eligibility checking, recommendation reporting,
and administrator support functions.


     INTERFACE FLOW DIAGRAMS




Narrative:
Future Path opens on a single Homepage that splits into two navigating systems, the Learner
Advisory System and System Staff Management System. The Management System is an
authenticated staff area behind a login that branches by role into Career Advisor and Administrator,
with Reports Generation being a shared function.

The Learner side is a six step or optional seven step linear capture system with validation loops that
converge on an Eligibility Results screen. From there, Learner can branch into Alternative Program
Recommendations and Funding Options that feed into a printable Advisory Summary Report.

The Advisor side is a lookup and view hub that can search through learner profiles and report data
and generate necessary reports to improve the system and reference data. The Administrator side
mirrors this hub pattern for Reference data maintenance that splits into individual maintenance
screens serving specific data categories needed in the system.

Assumptions:

- Faculty & Department Selection has no dedicated error screen as it will be selection based rather
than text entry input, so there’s less room for invalid input.

- The shared Login condition covers both Advisor and Administrator as their branching will be done
internally via the user’s role rather than two separate forms.

- Sessions Dashboard is a hub, not a forced sequence. The Advisor can search once and can jump
between Learner Profile, Session Info and Reports without researching again.

- Advisory Reports Dashboards shortcuts straight into the Summary Report. The Advisor will be able
to get a summary report of all learners' Advisory reports or a selected range of students.

- The Administrator’s Maintenance Dashboard groups all categories under one dashboard but keeps
its own independent screen with executable controls. There are separate categories for easier
maintainability.


    SCREEN STANDARDS
The standards below are applied to every screen listed in 2.1. They are fixed before development,
and every rule is expressed in terms of the .NET Framework Windows Forms controls and
regulations. A screen built to this standard can be designed using Visual Studio without custom
painting libraries.

2.2.1) Form Layout and Positioning

    •   Fixed size Forms, FormBorderStyle = FixedSingle, MaximizeBox = false, StartPosition =
        CenterScreen, AutoScaleMode = Dpi.
        Three sizes only: wide 1200 × 800 px for capture and record screens, standard 1000 × 720 px
        for outcome, hub and selection screens. All fit a 1400 × 800 display without scrolling.
    •   Mandatory zones on every screen. Caption bar reading "Future Path <screen name>",
        navigation rail docked left, title band carrying the screen name, action band docked bottom
        with navigation button, and an overall status band for the learners progress.
    •   Conditional zones. A learner context strip where the screen concerns one learner, an amber
        notice panel where something is still outstanding, and up to four running-figure tiles where
        derived values help. Each is hidden entirely rather than shown empty.

2.2.4) Fonts and Headings

One typeface throughout, Segoe UI, which ships with Windows, so no font needs installing on a
laboratory machine and no substitution can break a layout. Consolas is used only for figures,
because monospaced digits stay aligned in a column. Hierarchy is carried by size and weight, never
by colour, and Semibold is used in place of Bold.

Screen Title – Segoe UI Semibold large (20pt) acting as screen name in the title band.
Section Heading – Segoe UI Semibold medium sized (12pt) in section head bars.
Body – Segoe UI (10pt) for context texts, and grid cells
Field Label – Segoe UI Semibold (12pt) labels above their control
Caption – Segoe UI (8pt) for helper texts and status band
Figures – Consolas (10pt) for marks, scores, dates and identifiers.

2.2.5) Field Labels

        •    Every input control has a visible label, positioned directly above the control and left-
             aligned.
        •    Labels are sentence cases with no trailing colon.
        •    Units and formats live in the label ie) Mark% and never as a grey placeholder inside the
             text fields.

    •   Blue tint – Used for banding, context strip fills and grid rows.

    •   Amber Text – Used to highlight outstanding data.

3.      DESIGN SEQUENCE DIAGRAMS

     CHECK DEGREE ELIGIBILITY AND GENERATE AN ADVISORY OUTCOME




Systems Specification for [Future Path Student Advising System]   21
INF2011S


Narrative:

The process starts when the learner selects the Check Eligibility button from the Faculty and
Department Selection screen, this generates the request for the learner ID and programme ID to be
sent to the controller. The controller fetches the learner details from the Learner Repository, and the
degree programme details from the Degree Programme Repository. If the required learner or
programme information is missing, the controller displays a message learner "More Information
Needed". Alternatively, if both the details are present, the controller retrieves the subjects captured
by the learner and their choice of faculty. Then it calculates the scores required to give out a
recommendation. The degree programme determines the match between the subjects and marks
and gives out a match result. The interest categories that are associated with the programme are
used by the learner to find the interest score, while the degree programme checks whether it
matches the learner's preferred faculty and gives out a faculty bonus. All this data is taken and
processed by the Recommendation Engine to produce an outcome for the learner. The controller
then saves a new advisory session and a new recommendation result, before sending the outcome
back to the screen, which displays it to the learner along with its explanation.

Systems Specification for [Future Path Student Advising System]   23

Narrative:

The sequence diagram begins where the learner chooses to view the alternative programmes in the
Eligibility Results screen, this triggers a request for the learner ID and excluded programme ID to be
sent to the controller. The controller then retrieves the learner's information from the Learner
Repository. If there are missing subjects or interests by the learner, the controller displays a message
to the learner "More Information Needed". Alternatively, the controller retrieves the learner's
subjects along with the faculty chosen and retrieves the candidate programmes list from the Degree
Programme Repository. For each candidate programme, it computes the scores to recommend. The
degree programme identifies the match between the subject and marks and gives a match result.
The interest categories of the programmes will be used by the learner to compute interest scores.
Whereas the degree programme validates whether the programme matches the faculty chosen by
the learner and gives a faculty bonus. All these pieces of information are then collected and analysed
by the Recommendation Engine to generate a result for each candidate. After scoring all the
programmes the Recommendation Engine ranks the candidates and the controller stores the ranked
list, which is sent to the learner to display the list of the alternative programme.

Narrative:

The boundary classes are the screens the user uses to access the system and form the presentation
layer. The controller classes form the business logic layer together with the recommendation engine
class that manages the scoring logic. The entity classes hold the data used by the system whereas
the data access classes are used to store and retrieve the data from the database. These entities
consist of the Learner, Degree Programme, Programme Requirement, Advisory Session, and
Recommendation Result. These classes handle the profile and subjects of the learners, qualifications,
their requirements and the results of the advisory sessions. The two classes, Match Result and
Advisory Outcome represent the outcomes for the sequence diagrams. They are both temporary
values that are used during the process of generating recommendations. The User Account class has
been left disconnected because the learners go through an entire advisory process without logging
in.

5.      ENTITY RELATIONSHIP DIAGRAM
Narrative:

Each faculty offers one or more-degree programs, and each program stipulates one or more
requirements, a required subject, a minimum mark, and optionally an accepted alternative. A
learner undertakes zero or more advisory sessions. Each session records the grade level, preferred
faculty and selected program, and captures the learner's subject marks and questionnaire responses

    BUILT-IN VALIDATION
    Validation is applied at two points for every rule below, once in the presentation layer (so the
    learner or staff member gets immediate feedback) and again in the business logic layer (so a rule
    can never be bypassed by a malformed or unexpected request from the interface layer):

    •   Marks must fall between 0 and 100.

    •   Required fields (e.g. grade, at least one subject and mark, a selected faculty or degree) may
        not be left blank before the learner can proceed.

    •   The same subject may not be captured twice within one advisory session, the business logic
        layer checks existing Learner Subject entries for the current session before allowing a new
        one to be added.

    •   Dates (such as a funding option's closing date) must be valid calendar dates, entered
        through a date picker control rather than free text.

    •   A degree programme must be linked to a valid, existing faculty and enforced by the foreign
        key between Degree Programme and Faculty, and by only offering existing faculties in the
        degree-maintenance form.

    •   Funding type may only be selected from the predefined set of values (e.g. Scholarship,
        Bursary, Financial Aid, External Funding), never entered as free text.


    INPUT INTEGRITY CONTROLS
    •   Value Limit Controls: Numeric fields such as marks are constrained to their valid range (0–
        100), checking the range of inputs for reasonableness.

    •   Data Validation Controls: Dates, numeric fields and contact details are validated against
        their expected format before being accepted; subjects, faculties, degrees, interest categories
        and funding types are selected from stored reference data (lookup lists) rather than typed.
        Together these ensure that specific data values entered are correct and that inconsistent or
        invalid values cannot enter the database.
    •   Completeness Controls: The interface will not allow a learner to proceed to the eligibility
        check, or an administrator to save a new record, while mandatory fields are empty —
        ensuring all required data has been entered.
    •   Field Combination Controls: The business logic layer rejects a second entry for a subject
        already captured in the same advisory session, ensuring data is correct based on the
        relationship between the learner, subject and advisory session records.

    •   Referential integrity: Foreign key relationships (Degree Programme → Faculty, Programme
        Requirement → Degree Programme, Learner Subject → Subject, Funding Faculty / Funding

 Types of tests                                           How it will be tested

 System testing                                           Test the complete Future Path system from start

11.        RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN DESIGN AND DEVELOPMENT
The Systems Design Specification provides the detailed design that was decided for the system Future
Path. The development of the system will follow the design choices that were done in the different
sections of this document. The developed system will correspond with the designs for the interfaces,
classes, database, sequence diagrams, reports, validation and control, and the access control that was
specified in this specification.


The Interface Design Specifications discussed in Section 2 illustrate the screens that the Learner, Career
Advisor and Administrator will interact with. This outlines the screens from the Homepage to Learner
Profile Capture, Subjects and Marks Capture, Interest Questionnaire, Faculty & Department Selection,
Eligibility Results, Alternative Programmes, Funding Options, Advisory Summary, the staff-facing
Sessions Dashboard and Reference Data Management screens. This section discusses the links between
these screens as well as the screen specifications such as the button positioning, colour usage, and error
messages. The class design is provided in Section 4. The section discusses the boundary classes behind
each screen, the controller classes responsible for implementing the business logic, and the entity and
data access classes responsible for storing and accessing the data. This section also describes the
Recommendation Engine that integrates the scores from the four categories into one result and its two
result classes of Match Result and Advisory Outcome.


The database structure is discussed in Section 5. This discusses the entities for storing the data of the
Learner, Degree Programme, Programme Requirement, Advisory Session and Recommendation Result,
as well as the funding and interest entities. The database is normalised in order to avoid any kind of
repetition and use of foreign keys to maintain relations such as the relation between the Degree
Programme and the Programme Requirements and the relation between the learner and the subjects
being captured. The sequence diagrams are discussed in Section 3. The sequence diagrams demonstrate
the interaction between the classes discussed in Section 4 as they perform the two processes of
assessing whether the learner qualifies for their chosen programme and generating an advisory
outcome and generating a ranked list of alternative degree recommendations when the learner does
not achieve a strong match.

Section 6 provides information about the report design, this includes the three types of reports that will
be produced by the system: the Learner Advisory Summary Report, Degree Interest and Eligibility Report
and the Learners Not Currently Eligible Report.


Validation and control are defined in Section 7. This includes ensuring that the captured data is validated
and controlled, such as the use of marks within a particular range, mandatory data fields and non-
duplicate subjects, and ensuring that the output is also accurate and sensible. Access control design is
explained in Section 8. It defines the authentication process where the Learner, Career Advisor, and
Administrator can access the functionality, and it allows the Learner the ability to finish an entire
advisory session without having to log in while the Career Advisor and Administrator must log in using a
User Account.
