PART A


Future Path Student Advising System Case Study

1. Business Background
Many Grade 11 and Grade 12 learners in South Africa face uncertainty when deciding what to study after
school. Learners are often interested in particular degrees or career paths but may not know whether their
current subjects, marks and interests align with the requirements of those qualifications. In many cases,
learners only discover that they do not meet the minimum requirements when they are already close to
applying, or after they have already submitted an application.

This problem is especially common among learners who do not have regular access to career advisors,
university open days or detailed admission information. Some learners rely on parents, teachers, friends, social
media or outdated information when making important study decisions. This can result in unrealistic study
choices, missed funding opportunities, late applications and poor alignment between the learner’s strengths
and their intended field of study.

To support learners earlier in their decision-making process, a new standalone Information System has been
proposed. The system will be called Future Path. Future Path is a student pathway advising system designed to
help Grade 11 and Grade 12 learners explore possible study options, check whether they appear to meet the
minimum criteria for selected degrees, identify gaps in their current academic profile, view relevant funding
information, and export an advisory summary that can be shared with a parent, teacher or career advisor.

The system is not intended to make official admission decisions. Instead, it provides preliminary guidance
based on locally stored degree information, subject requirements, interest categories, funding options and
rule-based recommendation logic.

2. Strategic Purpose of Future Path
The purpose of the system is to provide learners with a structured and accessible way to understand their
possible study pathways before they formally apply to university. The system aims to:

•   help learners understand whether their school subjects and marks align with selected qualifications;
•   suggest alternative degrees where the learner’s current academic profile may be better suited;
•   match learner interests to faculties, degrees and career areas;
•   identify subject or mark gaps that the learner may need to improve;
•   provide faculty- or degree-related funding information;
•   generate an exportable advisory report that can be shown to parents, teachers or career advisors;
•   support more informed, realistic and timely study decisions.

The system will be developed as a standalone Windows Forms-based desktop application with a local
relational database. It must follow a three-tier architecture consisting of a presentation layer, business logic
layer and data layer. The system must not rely on a web server, cloud service, internet connection or external
APIs.

3. Operating Details
Future Path will be used by learners, school career advisors and system administrators.

Learners will enter their current school grade, subjects, marks, areas of interest and preferred faculty or
qualification. Future Path will then use locally stored rules and data to generate an advisory outcome. This
outcome may indicate that the learner is a strong match, a possible match, or not currently eligible for the
selected qualification.

For example, a learner may be interested in a Bachelor of Commerce degree but may not know whether
Mathematics is required, whether Mathematical Literacy is accepted, or what level of performance is
expected. Another learner may be interested in a Science-related qualification but may not know whether
Physical Sciences is required. Future Path should guide learners by comparing their information against the
qualification requirements stored in the system.

Future Path should also allow learners to complete a short interest questionnaire. The learner’s responses will
be used to calculate an interest alignment score. This score will help the system suggest study areas that may
be suitable for the learner’s interests.

Future Path should also ask whether the learner would like more information about possible funding options.
If the learner selects this option, the system should display funding information linked to the selected faculty,
qualification or study field. This may include scholarships, bursaries, financial aid options, merit awards, or
other sample funding opportunities stored in the database.

Once the learner has completed the advising process, the system should allow the learner to export a
summary report. This report should include the learner’s selected degree, subject and mark summary,
eligibility outcome, interest match result, identified gaps, possible alternative qualifications, and relevant
funding options.

4. Important System Constraint
Future Path must include recommendation and advising functionality. However, the system must not use
ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, online APIs, cloud-based AI tools or any external services.

The recommendation and advising functionality must be implemented using local system logic, such as:

•   rule-based eligibility checking;
•   keyword or category matching;
•   interest scoring;
•   weighted recommendation scores;
•   subject and mark comparison;
•   ranking of possible degrees;
•   gap analysis;
•   database-driven advisory messages.

The system should therefore simulate an intelligent advising experience while remaining fully standalone and
database-driven.

5. Existing Problem Context
Currently, many learners make study choices using fragmented information. A learner may check a university
website, ask a teacher, speak to a parent, attend an open day, or rely on advice from peers. However, this
process is often inconsistent. Learners may not understand how minimum requirements work, may confuse
admission requirements with selection likelihood, or may overlook funding opportunities.



                                                                                                               5
Schools may also have limited career guidance capacity. One teacher may be responsible for advising many
learners, and it may not be practical to manually assess each learner’s subjects, marks, interests and possible
degree options. Learners may also be embarrassed to ask questions or may not know what questions to ask.

Future Path is intended to support this process by providing a structured, repeatable and transparent advising
tool.

6. Typical Business Processes

6.1 Learner Profile Creation
A learner starts by creating a basic profile. The system should allow the learner to enter their name, current
grade, school, province and contact details. The system may also allow the learner to continue without
entering sensitive personal information, depending on the design decisions made by the project team.

The learner must indicate whether they are currently in Grade 11 or Grade 12. This is important because Grade
11 learners may be using projected or current marks, while Grade 12 learners may be using final or most
recent results.

6.2 Capturing Subjects and Marks
The learner must enter their school subjects and current marks. The system should allow the learner to add
multiple subjects, such as Mathematics, Mathematical Literacy, Physical Sciences, Life Sciences, Accounting,
Business Studies, Geography, History, English and other relevant subjects.

The system must validate marks to ensure that they are within a valid range. For example, a mark cannot be
below 0 or above 100. The system should also prevent a learner from entering the same subject more than
once for the same advising session.

6.3 Selecting a Preferred Faculty or Qualification
The learner can either select a preferred faculty first or search for a specific qualification. Faculties may include
Commerce, Humanities, Science, Engineering, Health Sciences, Law, Education or other locally stored faculty
options.

 The learner may select one degree programme for assessment during an advisory session. The system may
recommend multiple alternative programmes. The system should retrieve the requirements for the selected
degree from the database and compare these requirements with the learner’s captured subjects and marks.

6.4 Completing an Interest Questionnaire
The learner should answer a short interest questionnaire. This questionnaire will help the system determine
whether the learner’s interests align with the selected qualification.

Example interest areas may include:

•   working with numbers and data;
•   solving technical or scientific problems;
•   helping people and communities;
•   writing, debating and analysing ideas;
•   working with business and entrepreneurship;
                                                                                                                    6
•      designing systems or technology solutions;
•      working in healthcare or life sciences;
•      understanding society, politics or law.

Each response should contribute to one or more interest categories. The system should use these responses to
calculate an interest alignment score for relevant degrees or faculties.

6.5 Checking Degree Eligibility
The system should compare the learner’s subjects and marks against the stored requirements for the selected
qualification.

The system should produce one of the following advisory outcomes:

    Outcome                                                Meaning
    Strong Match                                           The learner appears to meet the minimum
                                                           requirements and has strong interest alignment.
 Possible Match                                            The learner meets some requirements, but has one
                                                           or more gaps or weaker alignment.
 Not Currently Eligible                                    The learner does not currently meet one or more
                                                           important requirements.
 More Information Needed                                   The system cannot complete the check because
                                                           required information is missing.
The system must clearly explain the reason for the outcome. For example, it should not only say “Not Currently
Eligible”. It should explain that the learner may be missing a required subject or that a mark is below the
required threshold.

6.6 Recommending Alternative Degrees
If the learner does not appear to meet the requirements for the selected degree, the system should
recommend possible alternatives. These recommendations should be based on the learner’s current subjects,
marks and interest profile.

For example, if a learner is interested in a qualification that requires Mathematics but only has Mathematical
Literacy, the system may suggest alternative qualifications where Mathematical Literacy is accepted,
depending on the locally stored sample data.

6.7 Viewing Funding Options
After the system displays the learner’s advisory outcome, the learner should be asked whether they would like
to view possible funding options related to the selected faculty or qualification.

If the learner selects yes, the system should display relevant funding options from the database.

    Linked faculty                                       Commerce

6.8 Exporting the Advisory Summary
The learner should be able to export an advisory summary. This report should be suitable for sharing with a
parent, teacher, school career advisor or for the learner’s own planning.

The export summary should include:

•     learner profile summary;
•     selected faculty or degree;
•     captured subjects and marks;
•     interest category results;
•     eligibility outcome;
•     explanation of gaps;
•     suggested alternative degrees;
•     relevant funding options;
•     date generated;
•     advisory disclaimer.

The export may be implemented as a printable report, PDF-style report, saved file or on-screen report,
depending on the implementation approach selected by the student team.

9. Recommendation Logic
The system should contain a recommendation engine. The recommendation engine does not need to use real
artificial intelligence or machine learning. It should be implemented using business rules and scoring logic,
comparable to rule-based Expert Systems.

A possible scoring approach is shown below:

 Score Component                                         Description
 Subject Match Score                                     Checks whether the learner has the required
                                                         subjects.
                                                                                                                  9
    Mark Match Score                                Checks whether the learner’s marks meet the stored
                                                    minimum values.
 Interest Match Score                               Compares learner interests with degree interest
                                                    categories.
 Faculty Preference Score                           Gives additional weight to degrees in the learner’s
                                                    preferred faculty.
 Overall Recommendation Score                       Combines the above scores into an overall advisory
                                                    result.
The system should show the recommendation in a way that is understandable to learners. For example:

            You are a possible match for this qualification. You meet the general subject
            requirements, but your Mathematics mark is below the suggested minimum. You may
            need to improve this mark or consider one of the alternative programmes listed below.


10. Reports
The system must generate reports that support learner advising, career guidance and system administration.
Reports may include summary reports, detailed reports, executive reports, operational reports and exception
reports.

At minimum, each project team must design and implement three meaningful reports. These reports should
be relevant to the purpose of the system and should use data stored in the local database.

10.1 Report 1: Learner Advisory Summary Report
This report is generated for an individual learner. It summarises the learner’s profile, subjects, marks, interest
results, selected qualification, eligibility outcome, recommended alternatives and funding options. This report
should be exportable so that the learner can share it with a parent, teacher or career advisor.

10.2 Report 2: Degree Interest and Eligibility Report
This report is generated by an administrator or career advisor. It provides a summary of advising activity across
learners. The report may include:

•     most selected faculties;
•     most selected degrees;
•     number of learners classified as strong matches;
•     number of learners classified as possible matches;
•     number of learners not currently eligible;
•     common subject gaps;
•     common mark gaps;
•     most requested funding categories.

This report can help schools or advisors identify where learners need more support.

10.3 Report 3: Learners Not Currently Eligible Exception Report
This report is generated by a career advisor or system administrator. It lists learners whose advisory outcome
is Not Currently Eligible or More Information Needed. The report helps identify learners who may need further
guidance, subject advice or alternative programme recommendations.

11. Input and Output Controls
The system should include validation and controls to reduce errors.

Examples of input controls include:

•   marks must be between 0 and 100;
•   required fields must not be left blank;
•   a learner cannot enter the same subject twice in one advisory session;
•   a degree must belong to a valid faculty;
•   a funding option must have a valid funding type;
•   closing dates must be valid dates;
•   administrator functions must require login.

Examples of output controls include:

•   advisory outcomes must include a disclaimer;
•   exported reports must show the date generated;
•   recommendations must show the reason for the outcome;
•   funding information must show whether it is sample information or requires verification;
•   inactive degrees or outdated funding options should not appear in learner recommendations.

12. Security and Privacy Considerations
The system should only collect information needed for advising. Since the system is designed for school
learners, the project team should consider privacy carefully.

The system should not require unnecessary sensitive personal information. Learner names may be optional if
the advisory session is only for guidance. Administrators and Career Advisors must authenticate before
accessing functionality associated with their respective roles. Appropriate role-based access restrictions must
be implemented.

Administrators should be the only users allowed to maintain degree requirements, funding information and
interest questionnaire data.

13. Future Requirements
The System may later be expanded to include additional features, such as:

•   support for multiple universities;
•   more detailed career pathway mapping;
•   parent or teacher login;
•   official application checklist generation;
•   reminders for application and funding closing dates;
•   integration with online university information sources;
•   multilingual support;
•   improved natural language questioning.

•   include a rule-based recommendation feature using local rules and scoring logic;
•   allow learners to capture subjects, marks and interests;

•   Include at least three appropriate reports, such as a summary report, detailed report, executive report,

The system must not connect to external AI tools, online APIs, cloud platforms or web servers.

10. Test Cases
Each group must prepare at least 5 test cases to demonstrate that the system meets the user requirements.

The test cases should include:

1. Create a learner profile and capture subjects and marks.

2. Check eligibility for a selected degree.

3. Generate alternative recommendations for a learner who does not meet requirements.

4. View funding options linked to a selected faculty or degree.

5. Export a learner advisory summary report.

The test cases do not need to be submitted with the system specification, but they must be available during
the demonstration and used to show that the system meets the required functionality.
